**This week**: ISM released the manufacturing index (yesterday); Labor department releasing September job openings (today) and October jobs report (Friday); Bank of England decides on UK interest rates (Thursday).

**ISM Manufacturing PMI**: Index **rose** from 54.5 to 55.5 in October 2026 (beating expectation of 54.6)

Each month the Institute for Supply Management polls supply managers at hundreds of U.S. factories in different industries on whether new orders, production, hiring, delivery times, and stockpiles are growing, shrinking, or flat versus the month before. 

Each category becomes an index based on the share of managers reporting growth + 1/2 *times* the share of managers reporting no change or flat. For example: if 40% of managers report growth, 50% report no change, and 10% report decline, the index is 40 + (50/2) = 65. 

* An index of 50 means every manager reported no change
* Above 50 means more firms are growing than shrinking
* Below 50 means more firms are contracting

55.5 is the average of five of these indices: *Production*, *New Orders*, *Supplier Deliveries*, *Employment*, and *Inventories*. A separate Prices Index tracks what factories pay for materials.

October is now the 10th straight month of producers reporting **expansion** going back to January 2026

* *Production* jumped 2.3 points to 59, its highest reading in 5 years
* *New orders* edged up to 55.5
* Derived from these numbers is the *Backlog of Orders*, which fell 1.8 points to 54.6

But, *Production* is starting to outpace new orders a bit, and manufacturers are borrowing growth from future months. A key distinction here is that it's not *demand* growth, they're borrowing from, but *supply* growth.

* *Supplier Deliveries* slowed for an 11th month
* *Employment* cooled to 52.4
* *Inventories* jumped +2.8 to 51.4, indicating hoarding of materials
* *Prices Index* sits at 75.2, meaning factories continue to pay more for inputs each month
* *Producer Sentiment* worsened for the 3rd straight month to 66% negative with pricing volatility in 62% of negative comments

If this were demand-driven strength driving production and prices, we'd see hiring accelerating alongside production instead of cooling. Instead, we see a slow down of deliveries for 11 straight months. We're seeing the inflationary mix of steady demand with a supply side struggling to keep up each month. 

That sets up the cliff: when the backlog finally runs out, production falls back to the pace of orders, which would otherwise imply a central bank rate cut. But with inflation running as high as it is, the Fed will have no room to cut rates to cushion the slowdown.

**WHAT TO WATCH: Backlog of Orders in November's report**
If that slides under 50, we've reached the cliff!
