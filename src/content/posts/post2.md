title: "The addictive loop of LLM-Driven Development"
date: 2026-10-30
category: "Development"
excerpt: "Building is Addictive"
---

Anyone who has worked in game development in the past 20 years is well versed with the concept of "intermittent rewards" or valuable assets that get distributed to players at sometimes unexpected, sometimes reliable times. Whether players know it or not, they are expecting these rewards to come at a regular cadence even if it doesn't *feel* like a regular cadence. Sometimes those rewards are surprisingly good, more often they're just a "steady currency" so that the player can keep up with the demands of the game. 

LLM-driven development feeds this same feeling. You pull the prompt (or more recently, seed the loop) and after an unpredictable period of time, the reward comes. Is it a surprisingly good end-to-end product with a modern design and robust data features? Or is it completely unusable and will require more handholding work than it would have required from the start?

LLM-driven development has come a long way since 2024 when your results will more often resemble the former, but the lazier you get in your late note coding sessions, the more likely you'll happen upon the latter and waste your evening getting the product on track towards your original intentions.
