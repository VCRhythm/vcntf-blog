The AI economy is often described as if the hard part were the software.

But the more expensive part may be the physical world underneath it:

- data centers
- chips
- power
- land
- cooling systems
- networking
- long-term contracts for electricity

All of that has to be paid for before the future arrives.

That is why AI is not just a technology story. It is a **capital-demand story**.

## The mechanism

AI companies need enormous amounts of capital.

They borrow, issue bonds, sign long-term leases, and build assets that may take years to produce revenue. That creates demand for savings. When many large borrowers compete for the same pool of savings, the price of savings rises.

That price is the interest rate.

This is the basic crowding-out mechanism:

> More borrowers bid for the same savings, so the cost of borrowing rises.

AI infrastructure makes that mechanism concrete.

## Why discount rates matter

A discount rate is the rate used to convert future money into today’s value.

The further away a cash flow is, the more a higher discount rate hurts it.

A simple comparison:

| Future cash flow | At 4% discount rate | At 5% discount rate | Loss in present value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $100 in 2 years | $92.46 | $90.70 | 2% |
| $100 in 20 years | $45.64 | $37.69 | 17% |

The same one-point rise in rates barely changes a two-year cash flow. It crushes a 20-year one.

That is why higher rates hurt long-duration assets hardest: companies whose value depends on distant profits.

## What the bond market is telling us

The evidence is not just theoretical.

- Alphabet’s borrowing spread over Treasuries widened from **63 basis points** to **85 basis points** earlier this year.
- The 10-year Treasury yield rose about **63 basis points**.
- Most of that rise came from the real rate, not inflation expectations.
- A 30-year Treasury auction cleared near **5.216%**, a multi-decade high.
- Total government and corporate bond issuance is on track to exceed **$7 trillion**.

Those are not just “rates went up” facts. They are signs that capital has become more expensive.

And if AI firms, hyperscalers, and the government are all borrowing at once, they are competing for the same pool of savings.

## Why Friday’s jobs report matters

The jobs report is usually read as a labor-market number. But it is also a capital-cycle number.

If output and investment keep growing while payrolls stay weak, the economy may be becoming more capital-intensive.

That matters because AI can show up in GDP and productivity before it shows up in broad employment.

A firm can:

- buy more compute
- automate more work
- keep output steady
- avoid hiring
- and still increase profits

That is not proof that AI is replacing workers. But it is a sign that growth may be coming from capital rather than labor.

## Who gains, who loses

The AI buildout does not affect everyone the same way.

**Likely winners**

- owners of scarce compute
- chipmakers
- power producers
- landowners near data centers
- infrastructure contractors
- specialized engineers
- firms that own the models or distribution

**Likely pressure points**

- smaller companies that need capital but cannot borrow cheaply
- households facing higher rates
- firms with long-duration business models
- workers whose tasks are automated or restructured
- governments trying to fund deficits at the same time

The buildout can make the economy more productive while also making it more unequal.

## The fragile assumption

The optimistic case assumes AI infrastructure eventually produces enough revenue to justify the upfront cost.

That is not guaranteed.

The risk is not only that AI fails technically. The risk is also that:

- capacity is built too early
- utilization disappoints
- power costs stay higher than expected
- customers do not pay enough to cover the capital
- financing costs rise faster than revenues
- the same crowded trade unwinds all at once

If that happens, the AI buildout stops being a growth story and becomes a balance-sheet story.

## The data gap

We can observe higher rates and heavy borrowing. It is harder to know exactly how much of the rate rise is caused by AI rather than by:

- federal deficits
- energy investment
- defense spending
- higher term premiums
- central-bank policy
- global demand for safe assets

The bond market tells us that capital is more expensive. It does not cleanly identify which borrower is responsible.

## What to watch next

For the AI buildout, the key indicators are not only model benchmarks.

Watch:

- data-center construction spending
- hyperscaler capex guidance
- power prices and grid constraints
- bond issuance and credit spreads
- utilization rates
- labor demand in affected industries
- wage growth for specialized technical workers
- and whether output rises faster than employment

The AI buildout is not a story about chatbots alone. It is a story about who can afford to build the future.

## One idea to carry away

When a technology promises future profits, someone still has to pay for the machines today.

If many firms try to buy the future at once, the price of the money needed to build it goes up.

That is why AI is not only a software story. It is a capital-demand story.

**The future is being bid for today, and the interest rate is the auction price.**
