A recap of last week's releases, which continue to emphasize the impact of tightening supply conditions

* ISM stats indicated an inflationary mix of currently increased production  to keep up with demand, but tightening supply conditions and low manufacturing employment that could lead to demand outstripping supply imminently

* The Bank of England follows the Fed's September rate hikes with hikes of their own, and indicated another increase may come in December if supply constraints (specifically oil) are not relieved by then. The Bank is attempting to rein in prices, but there's only so much it can do if the shortage keeps squeezing supply

* October's US Jobs report showed added jobs *tripling* against forecasts so at least labor is taking some of the share of production increases, at least in the short term. Don't forget though that ISM indicated that employment in manufacturing was slowing, and that was echoed by the Jobs report which primarily showed hiring in business and professional services

Output is up, hiring concentrated in white collar sectors which will only keep demand running hot, and supply continues to tighten.

The macroeconomic factors to watch now that could sway the Fed's decision to hike:
* Whether the US stock market recovers from last week's tech-led fall. A recovering market would tell the Fed that demand is still running hot — the wealth effect keeping spending ahead of constrained supply while a falling one tightens financial conditions on its own and does some of the Fed's cooling for it.
* October CPI (Tuesday)and PPI (Friday), which will be the Fed's first hard read on whether prices are being affected by the supply squeeze
