## The demand for savings

![[Pasted image 20261106100800.png]]

Last week resulted in momentum stocks giving back nearly all of this year's gains. The chart above tracks two baskets since November 2025: value and dividend payers are up 35.8%, momentum just 2.0%. Momentum peaked in June and again in September, the second peak weaker than the first, and the spread has swung back toward value.

AI companies need more and more capital for land, compute, and electricity so they borrow, issue bonds, sign long-term leases, and build assets that may take years to produce revenue. When many large borrowers compete for the same pool of savings, the price of savings rises, and that price is the interest rate. Alphabet's spread over Treasuries widened from 63 to 85 basis points this year, which is investors charging a specific borrower more *compounded* by the whole sector issuing debt at the same time. 

The market is taking notice of this borrowing price on companies' bottom lines. 

## Borrowing is running hot, hot, hot

- The 10-year Treasury yield rose about 63 basis points and most of that rise came from the real rate, not inflation expectations

- A 30-year Treasury auction cleared near 5.216%, a multi-decade high

- Total government and corporate bond issuance is on track to exceed $7 trillion

## The Fed's upcoming rate decision

The Federal Reserve's target rate does not set any these yields directly. Bond yields and credit spreads are priced by lenders in the market. Since AI companies are the biggest borrowers, this *looks* like AI is the primary cause.

But AI spend is not the only reason money is becoming more valuable. Governments are spending more on energy and defense, which leads to widening deficits, all while inflation remains above target due to demand outpacing supply. 

All of which has led to signs that policy will continue to move: the Fed raised in September, held at 3.75% to 4% in October over two hawkish dissents. But a third, Governor Christopher Waller, has now since said he would back a quarter-point hike in December and more if inflation comes in hot. Inflation looks to be coming in hot this week-- watch the CPI and PPI.

If lending rates are being pushed by expected policy as well as by borrowers competing for savings, the two are hard to separate. The AI buildout lifts real rates through demand for capital while governments spending more on energy and defense lift inflation. 

The next question is whether that spread keeps widening if the Fed moves in December.
