Bloomberg’s “London Takes a Hit as Super Rich Worth $160 Billion Exit UK” by Benjamin Stupples on October 4, 2026 uses statistics as a scare tactic against tax changes that ask for billionaires to pay their share. His argument is that by asking these billionaires to contribute to society, countries are losing investment and business within their borders. And this is all Labour’s fault because they ended a “special system” (that the Conservative government had also planned to end) that allowed individuals to avoid UK taxes on their overseas wealth. 14 individuals are listed with assets totaling $160 billion who have left the UK.

Then, the statistic thrown out to complete this scare: **the top 1% of UK earners account for about 27% of all income tax revenue**

This is what’s not said:

* These 14 individuals account for at most 0.5% of overall *income* tax revenue. The top 1% of UK income taxpayers is a cohort of roughly 340k individuals who earn 180k+ per year
* Income tax is roughly *a quarter* of the UK government’s total tax take
* The article is about individuals leaving England because they *weren’t* previously paying taxes on their assets as non-domiciled residents and never states how much, if any, they were contributing

A more honest statistic assuming these particular individuals paid any taxes at all: **the top 0.00004% of UK earners account for about 0.125% of all tax revenue**

Unfortunately, these are the kinds of articles that lead to a wealth tax being defeated in California. They're still very effective being scary. There *is* a conversation to be had about the transferability of wealth into and out of countries, but I wish it was had using real statistics and modeled impacts.
